The Poisoned Download

A two-minute story about a hacked website, a swapped installer, and the one place the attacker could not reach.

Scroll down
1

Maya needs a calculator for her shop

She downloads Calc 2.4 from the project's own website. She is careful, so she compares the checksum printed beside the button. It matches.

2

03:12. Somebody else visits the website

An attacker takes over the download page. He swaps the installer for one with a little extra inside, and he edits the checksum next to it, in one go.

3

Every check still passes

The next customer is careful too, and the checksum matches, because the website held both the file and the proof. Whoever controls the website controls both.

This has happened to real projects. In 2016 the Linux Mint website and the Transmission website both served poisoned downloads after a break-in.

4

Now the same night, with a seal

When the maintainer shipped 2.4, she sealed the file list in a Bitcoin Cash transaction that only her project's token can make. The attacker can still take the website. He cannot edit the seal.

5
